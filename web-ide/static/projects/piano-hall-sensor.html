Piano Hall Sensor

Move a magnet toward or away from the KY-024. The AVR-MT128 measures the analog Hall voltage, quantizes the field change into musical notes, displays the note on its LCD, and plays it on the onboard buzzer.

Physical connector locations

Where are those pins?

All photos show the component side of the AVR-MT128; the LCD is on the opposite side. The red silkscreen numbers are the authority for orientation.

Do not count from the left.On EXT1 and EXT2, pin 1/2 is the rightmost pair in these component-side photos. Power off before moving any connection.
5 V versus 3.3 V

No divider is needed here

The AVR-MT128 runs its ATmega128 and ADC from 5 V. The KY-024 is also powered from that same 5 V rail, so its A0 signal remains within the ADC's 0–5 V measurement range. Adding a divider would unnecessarily throw away one third of the ADC range.

ADC count ≈ A0 voltage ÷ 5 V × 1023
Only for a 3.3 V ADC

Optional divider example

If this sensor were powered from 5 V but connected to a different controller whose ADC maximum is 3.3 V, use:

KY A0— 10 kΩ —●—3.3 V ADC● — 20 kΩ — GND
5 V × 20 kΩ ÷ (10 kΩ + 20 kΩ) = 3.33 V

Do not install this divider on the MT128 project. The MT128 ADC is already a 5 V ADC.

Step-by-step

  1. Remove all power.

    Disconnect the AVR-MT128 barrel supply and programmer before moving wires.

  2. Confirm the KY-024 labels.

    With the Hall element at the top and header at the bottom, this documented module reads A0, G, +, D0 left-to-right. Clone layouts may differ; follow the silkscreen on your module.

  3. Join ground first.

    Connect KY G to AVR-MT128 EXT2 pin 1 GND.

  4. Connect the analog signal.

    Place 1 kΩ in series between KY A0 and ADC header pin 3 (ADC0/PF0). Optionally place 100 nF from ADC0 to ground near the MT128.

  5. Connect 5 V.

    Connect KY + to AVR-MT128 EXT2 pin 2. Do not insert the 10 kΩ/20 kΩ divider described for 3.3 V controllers.

  6. Optionally connect D0.

    Connect KY D0 to EXT1 pin 12 (PD5) only if threshold events are useful. The supplied piano firmware uses A0 and does not need D0.

  7. Inspect before power.

    Check that + is not connected to G, A0, or D0. Confirm the module orientation one more time.

  8. Program safely.

    For the ISP500-TINY: power the programmer first, then power the MT128, verify the signature, compile, and flash.

  9. Calibrate with no magnet nearby.

    On startup the firmware averages 64 ADC samples. Keep the magnet away until the LCD leaves “Calibrating...”.

  10. Test and tune.

    Move the magnet slowly. If notes change too easily or not enough, adjust dead_zone and step in the firmware. The blue KY potentiometer affects D0 only, not A0.

Build and program

The source is part of the editable project tree.

ADC display test

Start with hall_adc_display.c. At boot and continuously afterward, the first LCD row reads AD Value:<0–1023>. Move the magnet and confirm the value changes before loading the piano behavior.

.\OlimexProgrammer.ps1 compile `
  -Source .\projects\piano-hall-sensor\hall_adc_display.c `
  -OutputDirectory .\build\piano-hall-sensor

Full piano behavior

  • Uses AVCC/5 V as the ADC reference
  • Averages 64 startup samples for the no-field baseline
  • Uses absolute displacement from baseline
  • Maps magnitude into C4 through C5
  • Displays the note and raw ADC value
  • Drives the onboard differential buzzer
.\OlimexProgrammer.ps1 signature
.\OlimexProgrammer.ps1 compile `
  -Source .\projects\piano-hall-sensor\piano_hall_sensor.c `
  -OutputDirectory .\build\piano-hall-sensor

.\OlimexProgrammer.ps1 flash `
  -HexFile .\build\piano-hall-sensor\piano_hall_sensor.hex
Before flashing

The existing MT128 program is backed up at build/atmega128/atmega128-flash-backup.hex. Flashing this project erases the existing flash but does not intentionally change fuses.
