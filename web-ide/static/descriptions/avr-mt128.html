What is on this board?

Every onboard component and external interface, with its purpose and documented ATmega128 connection.

Development board

AVR-MT128

A 5 V ATmega128 development and demonstration board with a display, human inputs, audible and relay outputs, serial communication, programming/debug headers, and expansion connectors.

Processor
ATmega128-16AI
Clock
16 MHz
Memory
128 KB flash · 4 KB SRAM · 4 KB EEPROM
Board supply
Regulated 5 V from 9 VAC or approximately 12 VDC input
16 subsystems
01
Compute

ATmega128 microcontroller

The main processor executes the firmware and controls every onboard peripheral. This board uses the industrial-temperature ATmega128-16AI package at 16 MHz.

  • 128 KB programmable flash
  • 4 KB SRAM and 4 KB EEPROM
  • Digital I/O, timers, ADC, SPI, JTAG, and two USARTs
DeviceATmega128-16AI
02
Display

16 × 2 character LCD

An onboard parallel character display for two rows of 16 characters. The factory firmware drives it in 4-bit mode and provides separate functions for commands, characters, and strings.

  • Data bus: PC4–PC7
  • Register Select: PC0
  • Read/Write: PC1
  • Enable: PC2
  • LCD_TR adjusts the display contrast/operating point
Firmwarelcd_initialize()lcd_write_character()
03
Human input

Five push buttons

Five active-low user buttons let firmware trigger board demonstrations and user actions.

  • B1 → PA0
  • B2 → PA1
  • B3 → PA2
  • B4 → PA3
  • B5 → PA4
Electrical behaviorPressed = logic low
04
Switched output

Electromechanical relay

A processor-controlled relay switches an electrically separate external circuit through screw terminals. The red status LED follows the relay state.

  • Control: PA6 / AD6
  • Contacts: Normally Open, Common, Normally Closed
  • Documented contact rating: 10 A at 250 VAC
Hazard: Mains voltage can kill and requires proper enclosure, clearances, wiring, fusing, and qualified handling.
FirmwarePORTA bit 6
05
Audible output

Onboard buzzer

An audible transducer driven from two opposing MCU outputs. Alternating the outputs creates a square-wave tone without a dedicated audio amplifier.

  • PE4 / OC3B / INT4
  • PE5 / OC3C / INT5
Factory behaviorAlternating differential drive while B4 is held
06
External device interface

Dallas touch-memory / 1-Wire port

This is a connector for an external Dallas/Maxim device. It is not an onboard temperature sensor. Olimex documents it as a Dallas touch-button port, commonly associated with iButton/1-Wire devices.

  • Signal: PA5
  • Connector pin 1: Dallas data
  • Connector pin 2: Ground
  • A compatible 1-Wire temperature sensor may be possible with suitable wiring and firmware, but none is fitted.
Onboard sensor?No
07
Communication

Two physical serial connections

The board exposes transmit and receive signals through a four-pin logic-level header and a female DB9 using the onboard RS-232 interface circuit.

  • 4-pin header: TXD, RXD, GND, +5 V
  • DB9: TXD pin 2, RXD pin 3, GND pin 5
  • Factory firmware uses 9600 baud, 8 data bits, 1 stop bit, no parity
CableStraight male-to-female DB9, not null modem
08
Analog input

ADC expansion header

A six-pin header exposes the analog reference, ground, and four analog-to-digital converter channels for external sensors.

  • AREF and GND
  • ADC0/PF0 through ADC3/PF3
MeasuresExternal analog voltage; no analog sensor is onboard
09
Pulse input

External frequency input

The FREQ connector accepts an external pulse signal. Jumper J routes it to one of two timer clock inputs.

  • Jumper 1–2: T2/PD7 (default)
  • Jumper 2–3: T1/PD6
  • Second connector pin is ground
PurposeCount or time external pulses
10
Clocking

Two crystal oscillators

The 16 MHz crystal clocks the CPU. A separate 32.768 kHz watch crystal supports asynchronous timer/real-time-clock timing.

  • 16 MHz: XTAL1/XTAL2
  • 32.768 kHz: TOSC1/PG4 and TOSC2/PG3
The watch crystal is not a separate battery-backed RTC chip.
CPU rate16 MHz
11
Power

5 V regulated supply

The barrel input feeds a diode bridge and 78L05 regulator to create the board's 5 V rail.

  • Input: 9 VAC or approximately +12 VDC
  • Typical board consumption: about 30 mA
  • The 78L05 is a low-current regulator; avoid substantial external loads
Output rail5 V
12
Reliability

Reset supervisor

A ZM33064 voltage supervisor resets the MCU when supply voltage is too low for reliable operation.

  • Typical threshold: 4.5 V
  • Prevents uncontrolled execution during a collapsing supply
DeviceZM33064
13
Programming

10-pin ICSP header

The In-Circuit Serial Programming header writes flash directly through RESET, programming data, and clock signals.

  • PDI/MOSI, PDO/MISO, SCK, RESET
  • 5 V target-sense and grounds
  • Used with the AVR-ISP500-TINY in this setup
Verified targetSignature 0x1E9702
14
Debugging

10-pin JTAG header

The Joint Test Action Group interface supports compatible in-circuit debugging and programming tools.

  • TCK, TMS, TDI, TDO, RESET
  • Target-voltage and ground references
  • Not pin-compatible with ICSP despite the same pin count
UseBreakpoints, stepping, and register inspection
15
Expansion

EXT1 and EXT2 headers

Two 14-pin headers expose otherwise unused MCU functions for custom hardware.

  • EXT1: interrupts, timer signals, USART clock, I²C/TWI
  • EXT2: SPI, PWM/timer outputs, Port G, external-memory signals
  • EXT2 pin 1: GND — upper contact of the rightmost pair beside ICSP
  • EXT2 pin 2: +5 V — lower contact of that same pair
  • The Hall module uses these EXT2 contacts for G and +; D0 uses EXT1 pin 12
Detailed mapEXT2 pinout
16
Indicators

Status LED and LCD trimmer

The red status LED indicates relay state. The LCD trimmer adjusts the display circuit.

  • Status LED follows relay operation
  • LCD_TR adjusts display contrast/operating point
Independent LED?No; it is tied to relay status
