Player Piano Controller

The Raspberry Pi selects a song and asks the Olimex controller to begin. The Olimex closes the piano's coin-switch circuit with its isolated relay, watches the bellows with a digital Hall sensor, releases the relay when the bellows arrive, then reports completion to the Pi.

Operating contract

One song cycle

The Olimex owns timing-critical hardware. The Pi owns song choice and higher-level commands.

  1. 1
    Pi receives a play request

    The Raspberry Pi 5 selects the requested piano song and opens /dev/serial0 at 115200 baud.

  2. 2
    Pi sends PLAY

    Pi GPIO14/TXD sends the newline-terminated PLAY command to MT128 USART1 RXD.

  3. 3
    Olimex closes the coin circuit

    The MT128 energizes its relay, joining relay contacts C and NO. This dry-contact closure simulates the piano's coin switch and starts playback.

  4. 4
    Bellows reach the Hall sensor

    The A3144-based module drives D0 into its detected state. The MT128 polls this signal rapidly and latches short events.

  5. 5
    Olimex releases the coin circuit

    The relay coil is immediately de-energized. C and NO open, ending the simulated coin short.

  6. 6
    Olimex reports COMPLETE

    MT128 USART1 sends COMPLETE to Pi GPIO15/RXD. The Pi prints the status and waits for the next song.

Verified on hardwarePi pins 8 and 10 passed a UART loopback. In the photograph, MT128 contacts left-to-right are red +5 V, green GND, blue RX, yellow TX. BEEP, LCD HI THERE, and return status STATUS READY were verified.
Why the divider remainsMT128 input pull-ups only establish an idle logic level; they do not convert voltage. Pi TX at 3.3 V can directly drive MT128 RX, but MT128 TX can reach 5 V and must be reduced before Pi RX. Disable software pull-ups on the UART pins.
TTL header onlyUse the MT128 4-pin TTL connector. Do not connect the Pi to the DB9 connector; DB9 uses true RS-232 voltages and requires an RS-232 transceiver.
Never connect MT128 +5 V to a Pi GPIO.The TTL header's leftmost red +5 V contact remains unused. The two boards are powered separately and share only signal ground.

Firmware integration

UART state machine

The onboard LCD and buttons provide a local EEPROM settings menu.

Integration statusUART is enabled at 115200 8N1. Pi-to-MT128 commands and MT128-to-Pi status have both been verified on the installed hardware.
IDLERelay open

Wait for a PLAY or RELAY_ON UART command while displaying Hall state.

PLAYINGRelay C-NO closed

After PLAY, energize PA6, send STARTED, and monitor Hall D0.

COMPLETERelay open

On Hall detection, release PA6 immediately, beep, and send COMPLETE to the Pi.

MENUBoard controls

Left/right cycle MAIN, AUTO RELEASE, and DEBUG. Middle toggles the displayed Boolean. With DEBUG ON, top latches the relay; bottom or Hall releases it.

Power-domain rule

The Pi and MT128 use separate power supplies and share ground only for logic. The piano coin circuit connects exclusively to the relay's isolated C and NO contacts.
